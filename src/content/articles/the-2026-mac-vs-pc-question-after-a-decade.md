I have used both Mac and PC professionally for ten years. The usage has been split roughly 50/50 across different jobs and projects. The split is what gave me perspective on both platforms. The perspective is what produced this comparison. The comparison is for 2026.

## The current state of both platforms

A quick snapshot.

**Mac (M-series).** Apple Silicon has been the platform for three years. The performance per watt is the best in the industry. The macOS is mature and stable. The integration with iPhone and iPad is excellent. The software ecosystem for creative work is unmatched. The hardware is premium-priced but holds value.

**PC (Intel/AMD + Windows 11).** Windows 11 has matured. The performance is competitive at the high end. The hardware variety is unmatched. The software compatibility is the broadest. The gaming experience is unmatched. The repairability and upgradability are better than Mac.

Both platforms are mature. Both have strengths. Both have weaknesses. The choice depends on what you do.

## What I actually use each for

The real-world split after ten years.

**Mac for:** Writing, design work, photo editing, video editing, audio editing, web browsing, email, calendar, notes. The Mac is my primary personal machine. The Mac is also my primary creative work machine. The Mac runs Logic Pro, Final Cut Pro, and Adobe Creative Cloud.

**PC for:** Software development (some tools are better on PC), gaming, Excel-heavy work, occasional Windows-only software testing. The PC is my secondary machine. The PC is also my work machine for software development.

The split is functional. The functional split is what works. The functional is what the comparison is about.

## What Mac does better

The specific advantages of the Mac.

**Performance per watt.** The M-series chips deliver performance that PCs cannot match at the same power draw. The performance per watt means the MacBook Pro runs cool and quiet even under heavy load. The quiet is what makes the MacBook Pro usable on planes and in cafes.

**Battery life.** The MacBook Pro M-series gets 12-18 hours of real-world battery life. The PC laptops at similar performance get 4-6 hours. The battery life is the difference between using the laptop for a full day or needing a charger. The charger is what most PC users carry.

**Display and speakers.** The MacBook Pro display and speakers are the best in any laptop. The display is color-accurate. The speakers are loud enough for casual listening. The display and speakers are what makes the MacBook Pro feel like a premium product.

**Build quality.** The MacBook Pro unibody construction is the best in any laptop. The unibody has held up over years of use. The unibody on most PCs starts to creak or show wear within 2-3 years. The build quality is what makes the MacBook Pro worth the price.

**Software ecosystem for creative work.** Final Cut Pro, Logic Pro, and the Adobe Creative Cloud apps are all better on Mac. The Mac version is what professionals use. The professional use is what produces the better software.

**Integration with iPhone.** The integration between Mac and iPhone is the best in the industry. The integration includes: Handoff, Universal Clipboard, AirDrop, iMessage, FaceTime, password sharing, and now Apple Intelligence features. The integration is what makes the Mac+iPhone combination compelling.

**Resale value.** The Mac holds its value better than any PC. The resale value after 3 years is typically 50-60 percent of original. The resale value for most PCs is 20-30 percent. The value retention is what makes the higher upfront price less painful.

## What PC does better

The specific advantages of the PC.

**Variety.** The PC market has laptops and desktops at every price point, every size, every configuration. The variety is unmatched. The variety is what makes the PC the right answer for specific needs (gaming laptops, workstations, ultra-light laptops).

**Repairability.** Most PC laptops are easier to repair and upgrade than Macs. The RAM and storage can be upgraded on many PC laptops. The upgrade is not possible on most Macs. The upgrade extends the life of the machine.

**Software compatibility.** Windows runs software that Mac cannot. The software includes: most enterprise software, most games, some professional tools (certain CAD software, certain financial software), and any software that has not been updated for Apple Silicon.

**Gaming.** Windows is the only real platform for PC gaming. The games run faster, the library is much larger, and the hardware is more powerful. The Mac is improving with Apple Silicon, but Windows is still the gaming platform.

**Price for performance.** At the same price point, a PC delivers more performance than a Mac. The €1,500 PC laptop will outperform the €1,500 MacBook Air for most tasks. The outperformance is what makes the PC the right answer for budget-conscious buyers.

**Ports.** Most PC laptops have more ports than Macs. The ports include USB-A, HDMI, Ethernet, and SD card readers. The ports on Macs are mostly USB-C. The ports make PCs more compatible with older peripherals.

**Customization.** PCs can be customized in ways Macs cannot. The customization includes: choosing the screen size, choosing the keyboard layout, choosing the exact configuration. The customization is what makes the PC the right answer for specific workflows.

## The 2026 specific recommendations

If you are buying a new machine in 2026.

**Buy a Mac if:**

- You do creative work (photo, video, audio, design)
- You value battery life and quiet operation
- You use an iPhone and want deep integration
- You write, take notes, or do general productivity
- You want a machine that holds its value
- You are willing to pay a premium for build quality

The Mac is most people’s primary machine. The Mac is the right answer for the use cases above. The Mac is what I recommend for most people I advise.

**Buy a PC if:**

- You game seriously
- You do software development with specific Windows-only tools
- You need a specific configuration (ultra-light, ultra-powerful, specific ports)
- You are on a budget and want maximum performance per dollar
- You need to run software that does not exist on Mac
- You want to upgrade components over time

The PC is the right answer for the use cases above. The PC is what I recommend for those use cases.

## The honest summary

The Mac vs PC question in 2026 is more nuanced than either camp admits. The Mac is the better choice for most people. The PC is the better choice for specific use cases. The choice depends on what you do.

The Mac advantages (battery, build quality, ecosystem, creative software) matter for most people. The Mac disadvantages (price, limited ports, gaming) matter for specific users. The PC advantages (variety, gaming, customization, software compatibility) matter for specific users. The PC disadvantages (battery, build quality, ecosystem) matter for most people.

The decision framework I use:

1. What is your primary use case?
2. What software do you need?
3. What is your budget?
4. What ecosystem are you in?
5. What matters most to you?

The five questions reveal the answer for most people. The answer is usually Mac. The answer is PC for specific use cases. The answer is what works.

The recommendation, if you are buying now: think about your actual work. The actual work is what determines the platform. The platform is what the work needs. The needs are what matter.

The Mac vs PC debate is mostly settled for most people. The debate continues for specific users. The continuing debate is what makes the platforms push each other forward. The forward is what the users benefit from.

## The future of both platforms

Where each platform is heading.

**Mac (M5, M6 expected).** The Mac will continue to lead on performance per watt. The Mac will likely close the gaming gap somewhat with continued Apple Silicon improvements. The Mac will deepen the ecosystem integration with Vision Pro (if that becomes mainstream) and continued iPhone integration. The Mac will likely keep the premium pricing. The pricing is what the value is.

**PC (Snapdragon X Elite, Ryzen AI 300).** The PC will continue to lead on variety and configurability. The PC will close the battery life gap with ARM-based Windows laptops. The PC will likely improve the AI integration with NPUs in every chip. The PC will likely remain the platform of choice for gamers and workstation users.

The two platforms are converging in some areas (battery, AI, performance) and diverging in others (ecosystem, gaming, configurability). The convergence is mostly about hardware. The divergence is mostly about software and ecosystem.

The choice in 2027 will likely be even more use-case specific. The case-specific is what makes the choice harder. The harder is what requires more research. The research is what this article was for.

The platform that wins for you is the platform that fits your actual work. The actual work is what determines the answer. The answer is what the research reveals. The reveals are the value of taking the time to think about this carefully.
