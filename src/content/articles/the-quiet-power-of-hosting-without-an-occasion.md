I used to only host for occasions. The occasions were birthdays, holidays, promotions, farewells. The hosting happened four or five times per year. The hosting was stressful. The hosting produced pictures, not relationships.

In 2024, I started hosting without occasions. The hosting was on random Tuesdays and slow Sundays. The hosting was for no reason. The hosting produced something I did not expect. The something was a closer circle of friends. The closer circle is what the experiment was for.

## What changed

The specific shift.

**No reason required.** I started inviting people over without a reason. The invitation was: “Come over Tuesday, I’m making pasta.” The invitation worked. The invitation worked because the people I invited also wanted connection. The connection is what we were missing.

**Two to four people.** The dinner parties used to be eight to twelve. The size was for occasions. The size made conversation difficult. The smaller dinner is two to four. The smaller dinner is more conversation. The conversation is what the dinner is for.

**No formality.** The dinner is casual. The casual means I cook in what I am wearing. The casual means people bring wine or nothing. The casual means we sit on the couch, not the dining table. The casual is what makes the dinner happen often.

**No pressure.** The pressure used to be on me to produce a great dinner. The great dinner was the point. The great dinner is now replaced with a good dinner. The good dinner is what I can produce on a Tuesday. The Tuesday is the point.

## Why this works

The deeper reason random hosting is more effective than occasion hosting.

**The occasion is for performance.** When you host a birthday or a holiday, the dinner is a performance. The performance is what guests experience. The experience is not the relationship. The relationship is what happens between performances.

**The random dinner is for presence.** When you host without a reason, the dinner is about being together. The being-together is the relationship. The relationship is what accumulates. The accumulation is what matters.

**Frequency beats intensity.** A weekly simple dinner produces more closeness than a quarterly elaborate dinner. The closeness comes from repetition. The repetition is the frequency. The frequency is what matters.

**Vulnerability requires safety.** The elaborate dinner creates performance pressure for everyone. The pressure makes people less open. The openness requires safety. The safety comes from simplicity. The simplicity is what enables the conversation.

**Connection requires regularity.** The friendships that matter are the ones with regular contact. The regularity is what the random dinners provide. The provide is what makes the friendships last.

## What the dinners look like

A typical random Tuesday dinner at my place.

**The invitation:** Sent by text on Sunday or Monday. The text is: “Tuesday? I’ll make pasta, bring wine if you want.” The simplicity is what makes the invitation easy to accept. The easy acceptance is what produces the yes.

**The prep:** I start cooking around 5pm if people arrive at 7. The cooking is usually one pasta, one salad, one bread. The bread is from the bakery. The bakery is what makes the dinner simple. The simple is what makes it sustainable.

**The arrival:** People arrive between 7 and 7:30. The arrival is staggered. The staggered means I am finishing cooking when the first person arrives. The finishing is part of the dinner. The part is what makes people feel at home.

**The dinner:** The dinner is two to four hours. The dinner is mostly conversation. The conversation is about what is happening in our lives. The lives are what we share. The sharing is what builds the relationship.

**The cleanup:** I clean up the next morning. The next-morning cleaning is part of the deal. The deal is what makes the hosting sustainable. The sustainable is what produces the frequency.

## What I learned about hosting

A few principles that emerged.

**The food matters less than you think.** I made elaborate dinners for years. The elaborate dinners were what guests complimented. The compliments were not what made the dinners good. The good was the conversation. The conversation did not require elaborate food.

**The wine matters more than you think.** The wine is what loosens the conversation. The loosening is what produces the good discussions. The discussions are what people remember. The wine is the social lubricant. The lubricant is part of the deal.

**The setting matters less than you think.** I have hosted in apartments of various sizes and styles. The hosting worked in all of them. The working was not about the setting. The working was about the people.

**The regularity matters most.** The regularity is what turns an acquaintance into a friend. The turning is what the experiment was for. The experiment produced the regularity. The regularity produced the friendships.

**The simplicity is what enables it.** The simple dinner is what I can produce weekly. The weekly is what produces the friendships. The friendships are the goal. The simple is what enables the goal.

## What I cook

A short list of what works for random hosting.

**Pasta.** The most reliable dinner. The pasta is one pot, one sauce, one hour. The sauce is whatever I have. The whatever is what makes it casual.

**Roast chicken.** The classic Sunday dinner. The chicken is one pan, one hour. The hour is when the conversation happens in the kitchen. The kitchen is the best room.

**Soup and bread.** The winter dinner. The soup is one pot, made in advance. The bread is from the bakery. The combination is what makes winter hosting easy.

**Tacos.** The casual Friday dinner. The tacos are a build-your-own situation. The situation is what makes it work for any dietary preference. The preference is what makes everyone happy.

**Curry.** The weeknight dinner. The curry is one pot, made with a paste. The paste is what makes it fast. The fast is what makes it sustainable.

The food is not the point. The food is the excuse. The excuse is what brings people over. The bringing-over is what produces the friendship. The friendship is the goal.

## Who I invite

The thinking behind the guest list.

**Not the same people every time.** The variety is what keeps the dinner interesting. The interesting is what makes people want to come back. The coming-back is what makes the friendship grow.

**People who like each other.** The liking is what I try to engineer. The engineering is based on past dinners. The past dinners tell me who got along with whom. The getting-along is what I optimize for.

**People at different life stages.** The variety is what makes the conversation rich. The richness comes from different perspectives. The perspectives are what produce the best discussions.

**People who are new to my life.** The newer people need the introduction. The introduction is what dinner provides. The providing is what helps new friendships form. The forming is the point.

**People I want to know better.** The wanting-to-know is what motivates the invitation. The motivation is what produces the follow-up. The follow-up is what makes the friendship develop.

## What the dinners produced

The unexpected benefits.

**A closer circle of about 12 people.** The 12 people are the ones I see regularly. The seeing is what makes them friends. The friends are what matter. The mattering is the value.

**Three people I now consider my closest friends.** The closeness came from the dinners. The dinners produced the conversations. The conversations produced the closeness. The closeness is the payoff.

**One new business collaboration.** The collaboration came from a dinner conversation. The dinner was the start. The start was the most useful byproduct.

**Better mental health.** The hosting improved my mood. The improvement came from the connection. The connection is what humans need. The need is what the dinners satisfied. The satisfaction is the value.

**A reason to keep my apartment nice.** The reason was having people over weekly. The weekly is what motivates the cleaning. The cleaning is what makes the apartment a place I want to live.

## The downsides

A few things to be honest about.

**It is more work than I expected.** The hosting is real work. The work includes cooking, cleaning, and emotional labor. The labor is what makes the hosting possible. The possible is what the experiment is for.

**Some people do not reciprocate.** The non-reciprocation is what some friends do. The doing is a small social cost. The cost is what I accepted.

**I rarely host elaborate dinners anymore.** The elaborate dinner was what I used to do. The doing was for occasions. The occasions are less frequent now. The frequent is what I prefer.

**The introvert drain is real.** The introvert drain comes after a dinner. The drain is what I recover from. The recovery takes about 24 hours. The 24 hours is the cost of being an introvert who hosts.

## The honest summary

Random hosting is one of the most valuable practices I have built. The practice produces deeper friendships. The friendships are what matter. The mattering is the value.

The shift from occasion hosting to random hosting was not just about frequency. The shift was about changing what hosting is for. The hosting is no longer for performance. The hosting is for presence.

The recommendation, if you want to try: start with one dinner per month. The one dinner is the start. The start is what produces the practice. The practice is what produces the friendships.

The food will not be perfect. The conversation will not always flow. The guests will not always click. The imperfection is part of the deal. The deal is what produces the closeness. The closeness is what the experiment is for.

The quiet power of random hosting is the regularity. The regularity is what makes it work. The work is what produces the friendships. The friendships are the goal. The goal is what the practice is for.
