In March 2025, I deleted every social media app from my phone. The deletion was for six months. The six months became permanent for most. The permanence is what this article is about.

The apps I deleted: Twitter (X), Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, LinkedIn, Reddit, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon. The deletion removed the apps from my phone. The phone still had a browser. The browser was for occasional lookups. The lookups are what I controlled.

Here is what I learned, what I kept, and what replaced the social apps.

## What I deleted

A short list.

**Twitter/X.** The most time-consuming app. The app I opened 30+ times per day. The app I deleted first because it was the worst.

**Instagram.** The second most time-consuming. The scrolling was the issue. The scrolling produced comparison. The comparison was the issue.

**TikTok.** The most addictive. The algorithm was what made it dangerous. The dangerous was the issue.

**Facebook.** The least used but most problematic for relationships. The problematic was what I wanted to avoid.

**LinkedIn.** The most professional but still problematic. The professional was the issue. The issue was the comparison.

**Reddit.** The most useful but also most time-consuming. The consumption was what I wanted to control.

**Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon.** The replacements for Twitter that were becoming time sinks in their own right. The right was what I wanted to avoid.

## The rules

The discipline that made the deletion work.

**No apps.** The apps were the problem. The problem was the immediate access. The access was what I removed.

**Browser only for specific purposes.** The browser was for looking up something specific. The specific was what I controlled. The controlled was the discipline.

**No checking “just to see.”** The “just to see” was the habit. The habit was the issue. The issue was what I broke.

**Replace the habit with something else.** The something else was the discipline. The discipline was what I focused on. The focus is what the experiment required.

## What I missed

The parts that were harder than I expected.

**Real-time information.** I missed knowing what was happening as it happened. The happening was the appeal. The appeal was what I gave up.

**Connection to distant friends.** I missed seeing what my distant friends were up to. The up-to was the social fabric. The fabric was what I missed.

**Discovering new ideas.** I missed the serendipitous discovery of new ideas. The discovery was the best part. The part was what I missed.

**Cultural moments.** I missed being part of cultural conversations in real time. The conversations were the social glue. The glue was what I missed.

**Professional networking.** I missed the LinkedIn connection. The connection was useful. The useful was what I missed.

## What I did not miss

The parts that were not as hard as I expected.

**The outrage cycle.** I did not miss the constant cycle of anger at current events. The anger was what I escaped. The escape was the value.

**The comparison trap.** I did not miss the constant comparison to other people’s curated lives. The lives were not real. The not-real was what I escaped.

**The performance pressure.** I did not miss the feeling that I should be posting about my own life. The posting was what I escaped.

**The dopamine.** I did not miss the constant dopamine hits from the feeds. The hits were the issue. The issue was what I escaped.

**The doomscrolling.** I did not miss the late-night doomscrolling. The scrolling was the issue. The issue was what I escaped.

## What replaced the social apps

The practices and tools that filled the gap.

**Reading.** The primary replacement. I read 47 books in 2025. The 2024 total was 31. The increase is the value of the time recovered.

**Long-form podcasts.** The secondary replacement. I listen to 5-7 hours per week. The listening is what I do during walks and chores.

**Newsletters.** The third replacement. I subscribe to 12 newsletters. The newsletters are email-based. The email is what I check at specific times.

**In-person social.** The fourth replacement. I see friends more often. The seeing is what I do instead of texting.

**Phone calls.** The fifth replacement. I call friends instead of texting. The calling is what I do instead of following.

**Hobbies.** The sixth replacement. I cook more, walk more, read more, write more. The more is what the time recovered enabled.

**iMessage group chats.** The seventh replacement. The chats are with close friends. The chats are not social media. The chats are real.

## What came back

A short list of what I allowed back, and why.

**Instagram (limited).** I keep Instagram for direct messages and to see stories from specific close friends. The access is via the browser. The browser is what I use twice a week. The twice is what I limit.

**LinkedIn (limited).** I keep LinkedIn for professional networking. The access is via the browser. The browser is what I use once a week. The once is what I limit. The networking is what I allow.

**WhatsApp (not social media, but worth noting).** I use WhatsApp heavily. The usage is for actual conversations. The conversations are what the app is for.

**iMessage (same).** The iMessage is what I use for American friends. The Apple ecosystem is what I am in.

**Substack (reading only).** I read Substack newsletters. The reading is what I do. The doing is what I allow.

## What did not come back

The apps I am not going back to.

**Twitter/X.** Not coming back. The platform is what I avoided. The avoided is what I prefer. The platform is now worse. The worse is what the experiment confirmed.

**TikTok.** Not coming back. The algorithm is too addictive. The addiction is what I want to avoid.

**Facebook.** Not coming back. The platform is not useful. The useful is what I want.

**Reddit.** Mostly not coming back. The occasional browser visit for a specific topic is what I do. The occasional is what I limit.

**Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon.** Not coming back. The replacements for Twitter are not what I need. The need is what the experiment addressed.

## The impact

The measurable and unmeasurable effects.

**Time recovered.** Roughly 1.5 hours per day. The 1.5 hours are what I now spend on reading, walking, and friends. The friends are what matter.

**Mental clarity.** Noticeable within two weeks. The clarity is what the absence of the feeds produced. The produced is the value.

**Sleep improvement.** The improvement was real within one week. The improvement came from no late-night scrolling. The scrolling was what I stopped.

**Relationships.** The relationships with close friends improved. The improvement came from more in-person time and more phone calls. The calls are what I prefer.

**Productivity.** The productivity at work improved. The improvement came from fewer interruptions. The interruptions were what I removed.

**Comparison anxiety.** The anxiety reduced significantly. The reduction is what I value most.

## What I tell people who want to try

A short guide.

**Delete the apps first.** The deletion is the easiest step. The step removes the immediate access. The access is what produces the consumption.

**Replace the habit.** The replacement is what makes the deletion sustainable. The sustainability is what makes the experiment work. The work is what produces the change.

**Set boundaries with the browser.** The browser is the loophole. The loophole is what I closed. The closing is the discipline.

**Give it 30 days.** The 30 days is enough to see most of the effects. The effects are what convince you to continue. The continuation is what produces the change.

**Do not delete your accounts.** The accounts are what you might want to come back to. The coming-back is what I allowed for two of them. The allowing is what made the experiment feel less permanent. The feeling was what made it easier to start.

## The honest summary

Deleting every social app was one of the most impactful changes I have made. The change was not ideological. The change was practical. The change came from noticing the cost of consumption.

The benefits are real. The benefits are: more time, better focus, better sleep, better relationships, less anxiety. The benefits are what the experiment produced.

The costs are real. The costs are: missing real-time information, missing distant friends, missing professional networking. The costs are manageable. The costs are what I accepted.

The shift is not for everyone. The shift works for me because I value focus and presence more than being on top of every development. The value is what the experiment was for.

The recommendation, if you want to try: start with one app. The app to start with is Twitter. The deletion is the easiest. The easiest is what produces the first win. The win is what produces the next deletion.

The social apps are tools. The tools are useful in moderation. The moderation is what most people cannot maintain. The cannot is what the experiment was for.

The experiment is permanent for most of the apps. The permanence is what the experiment produced. The produced is what I value. The value is what the change is for.
