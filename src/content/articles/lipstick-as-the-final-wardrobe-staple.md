I have been wearing lipstick for twenty years. I have owned probably a hundred tubes in that time. The current count is four. The current four are the ones that have stayed, the ones that work with everything I own, and the ones I have replaced when they ran out rather than reaching for something else.

This is not a lipstick review post. It is the case for treating lipstick as a wardrobe staple rather than a beauty product. The difference is significant. A wardrobe staple is a piece you keep because it works. A beauty product is a piece you buy because someone convinced you to. The lipstick I wear now has earned its place in the wardrobe, not in the makeup drawer.

Here is what I have learned, what I keep, and the small framework that has eliminated the rest.

## What lipstick actually does

The function of lipstick is to give color and definition to the mouth. That is the whole function. The mouth is one of the focal points of the face. The color of the mouth affects how the whole face reads. A bright red mouth reads as confident, dressed, finished. A nude mouth reads as casual, neutral, less defined. A bare mouth reads as unconsidered, in the sense of "no opinion about color."

The point of lipstick is not to enhance the face. The point is to make a statement about color. The statement can be "I am dressed up today" (red), "I am dressed down today" (nude), or "I have no opinion" (nothing). Each of these statements is appropriate in different contexts.

The mistake most people make is owning too many tubes and choosing between them based on outfit, mood, or trend. The mistake is the choice itself. A wardrobe staple is worn without thinking about it. The lipstick I wear is the lipstick I reach for without consulting the mirror first.

## The four lipsticks I keep

The set has been refined over the last five years. None of these are affiliate picks. None are paid endorsements. They are the four tubes I have bought, worn through, and replaced.

**A nude-mauve everyday.** This is the default. The color is a brownish-mauve that is close to my natural lip color but slightly more saturated. It works with most outfits, most contexts, and most makeup looks. The finish is satin or creamy matte — not too glossy, not too dry. The brands I have used: Charlotte Tilbury Pillow Talk (€32), MAC Modesty (€28), Bobbi Brown Bare (€32). The current tube is Charlotte Tilbury. I replace it when it runs out, which is about every six months at daily use.

**A red for dressed-up days.** This is the lipstick for evenings, weddings, dinners, and any context where I want the mouth to read as deliberate. The color is a blue-red (more flattering on cool skin tones) with a satin finish. The brands I have used: MAC Ruby Woo (€28), Chanel Rouge Allure Velvet in Rouge Profond (€42), Tom Ford Lip Color in Scarlet Rouge (€50). The current tube is Chanel. It lasts about three months because I do not wear it daily.

**A deeper berry for autumn and winter.** This is a dark berry-red (almost wine-colored) that I wear with darker clothing in colder months. The finish is creamy, the texture is forgiving, the wear time is about four hours without touch-up. The brands I have used: MAC Diva (€28), Charlotte Tilbury Glowing Jen (€32), NARS Dolce Vita (€30). The current tube is Charlotte Tilbury.

**A tinted balm for no-makeup days.** This is the tube that lives in my bag. The color is barely there — a hint of tint, a hit of moisture, a soft sheen. The brands I have used: Clarins Lip Comfort Oil (€25), Dior Lip Glow (€36), fresh Sugar Lip Treatment (€26). The current tube is fresh Sugar. It lasts about two months at daily use.

Total number of tubes: four. Total cost-per-cycle: about €130. Total cost-per-year: about €200. The number of decisions saved per morning: one.

## What I let go

A partial list of the categories I no longer buy.

**The "new shade every season" trap.** The brands that release four new limited-edition shades every fall and spring, all of which are marketed as "the must-have shade of the season." I owned probably twenty of these between 2010 and 2020. None of them were good enough to replace the tubes I already had. Most were discontinued within two years. The new-shade-every-season trap is a marketing strategy that targets the wardrobe mentality in reverse — making you replace what you have rather than keeping what works.

**The "every occasion needs a different shade" trap.** The makeup artists who insist you need a daytime nude, an evening red, an autumn berry, a winter plum, a spring coral, a summer coral, a date-night red, a work red, a Sunday red. None of this is necessary. The four tubes I keep work for every occasion. The right red works with one face and one wardrobe. The right nude works with the same face and the same wardrobe.

**The "lipstick sets" trap.** The gift sets, the holiday collections, the limited-edition trios. Most of the lipsticks in these sets are colors I would not have bought individually. The bundled price is rarely a real discount. The result is a drawer of tubes I do not wear, which is exactly the problem the lipstick-as-wardrobe-staple model is designed to eliminate.

**The "skincare lipsticks" trap.** The hyaluronic-acid-infused lipsticks, the peptide-loaded lipsticks, the ceramide-rich lipsticks. Most of these are regular lipsticks with a small amount of an active ingredient that does not meaningfully change the product. The skincare lipstick is mostly a marketing claim. The lipstick is lipstick. The skincare is skincare.

## How to find your four

The framework I would recommend for someone starting from a drawer of too many tubes.

**Step one: identify your everyday color.** Wear nothing but a nude for two weeks. Notice which color you reach for. The one you reach for is the everyday. Replace it when it runs out. Do not buy a new one until you are sure.

**Step two: identify your dressed-up color.** Wear nothing but a red for two weeks. Notice which red you like. The one you like is the dressed-up color. Replace it when it runs out.

**Step three: identify the season-color, if any.** If you live in a climate with strong seasonal variation, you may want a deeper color for autumn and winter. If you do not, skip this category.

**Step four: identify your tinted balm.** The tube that lives in your bag for no-makeup days. This can be a tinted balm, a lip oil, a sheer lipstick. The point is that it is not "lipstick," exactly. It is closer to skincare with a hint of color.

The total number is four. The total cost-per-year is about €200. The number of decisions saved per year is approximately 365. The cost-per-decision-saved is about €0.55. By any reasonable calculation, this is a worthwhile trade.

## The cases against

The case against treating lipstick as a wardrobe staple is mostly a case for variety. Variety has its own value. Some people enjoy the daily decision. Some people enjoy the act of trying new shades. Some people have the budget to support a rotation of twenty.

These are valid reasons to own more than four tubes. The wardrobe-staple model is not the only model. It is the model for people who want the daily decision to be simple. If you do not want it to be simple, the rotation model works fine.

The other case against is that lipstick is a beauty product, not a fashion product. The lipstick I wear does change how my face reads, but it does not change how my clothes read. The lipstick is on the face. The clothes are on the body. The categories are different.

This is a fair point. The lipstick-as-wardrobe-staple framework is borrowed from fashion thinking. The borrowed framework works because the consumption problem is the same: too many options, too many decisions, not enough clarity about which ones earn the space.

## How to wear it

The application matters less than the choice. The basics, in case the basics are unclear.

**Prep.** Apply lip balm 10 minutes before lipstick. Blot off excess. The lipstick adheres better to a hydrated but not glossy surface.

**Apply.** Use the bullet directly, or use a lip brush for more precision. For the everyday nude, the bullet is fine. For the red, a brush gives more control.

**Blot.** Press a tissue between lips after application. This sets the color and removes excess. Reapply once for depth.

**Touch up.** Carry the tube. Touch up after eating. The color wears off through the center of the lips first.

**Remove.** At the end of the day, remove with micellar water or a cleansing oil. The skin around the mouth is thin and reactive. Harsh removers cause irritation.

The application is the same whether the lipstick costs €6 or €60. The expensive lipsticks have better packaging, better textures, and better longevity. The application does not change.

## The honest summary

Lipstick is the only beauty category I treat as a wardrobe staple. I keep four tubes. I replace them when they run out. I do not buy new shades because a magazine tells me to. I do not buy limited editions because the packaging is pretty.

The case for the four-tube model is the same case as the capsule wardrobe. The decision overhead is reduced. The cost-per-cycle is predictable. The mental model is simple. The face looks the same most days, which is a feature, not a bug.

The case against is that variety has its own value, and that beauty is not fashion. The trash-me model is the right one for some people. The four-tube model is the right one for me.

If you want to try the four-tube model, the path is straightforward. Wear what you have for a few weeks. Notice which ones you reach for. Keep those. Let the rest go. The four tubes that earn their place are the ones that work.
