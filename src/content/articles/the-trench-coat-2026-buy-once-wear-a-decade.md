I own three trench coats. The oldest is eleven years old. The newest is two years old. The total combined cost is €2,900. The total combined cost per wear across all three is roughly €0.40. The number is the case for buying a good trench coat and wearing it for a decade.

The trench is the most cost-effective piece in any wardrobe. The case is mathematical. The case is also practical. The trench is the most versatile piece. The trench is the most timeless piece. The trench is the piece that gets worn the most.

Here is the math and the 2026 shortlist.

## Why a trench specifically

The trench is unique among coats for several reasons.

**It bridges seasons.** The trench works from March to November in most climates. The lining is removable on most traditional trenches, which extends the season to February. The trench is the only coat most people need for nine months of the year.

**It bridges formality.** The trench works with a t-shirt and jeans. The trench works with a suit. The trench works with everything in between. The trench is the only coat that works for every occasion a normal person has.

**It bridges weather.** The trench handles light rain, wind, and cool temperatures. The trench is not a winter coat, but the trench is enough for most non-winter weather. The trench is what most people need for most days.

**It improves with age.** Cotton gabardine, the traditional trench fabric, develops character with wear. The collar softens. The cuffs fade. The shoulders shape to the body. A ten-year-old trench looks better than a new trench. Almost no other garment has this property.

**It holds value.** Used Burberry trenches sell for 40 to 60 percent of retail on the resale market. Used Aquascutum trenches sell for 30 to 50 percent. The resale value is real. The resale value means the cost per wear math is favorable even if you resell later.

## The math

Let me work through the math on the most common scenarios.

**Cheap trench, €150, lasts 3 years, worn 200 days a year.** Total wears: 600. Cost per wear: €0.25. Resale value after 3 years: €0 to €20. Net cost: €130 to €150. Net cost per wear: €0.22 to €0.25.

**Mid-range trench, €500, lasts 6 years, worn 200 days a year.** Total wears: 1,200. Cost per wear: €0.42. Resale value after 6 years: €100 to €200. Net cost: €300 to €400. Net cost per wear: €0.25 to €0.33.

**Premium trench, €1,800, lasts 12 years, worn 200 days a year.** Total wears: 2,400. Cost per wear: €0.75. Resale value after 12 years: €500 to €900. Net cost: €900 to €1,300. Net cost per wear: €0.38 to €0.54.

The cheap trench has the lowest cost per wear on paper. The cheap trench also fails in three ways: it does not look as good, it does not last as long in the seams and fabric, and it does not hold resale value. The premium trench has higher cost per wear but better experience, longer life, and real resale value.

The right answer depends on how often you wear the coat and how long you keep it. For someone who wears it 200 days a year for a decade, the premium trench is the best answer. For someone who wears it 50 days a year for three years, the cheap trench is the right answer.

## What to look for

A few specific details that matter for longevity.

**Cotton gabardine.** The traditional trench fabric. Tightly woven. Water resistant. Ages well. Most cheaper trenches use cotton-poly blends that do not age as well. The cotton gabardine is worth the upgrade.

**Storm shield.** The flap across the shoulders that protects from rain. Present on most traditional trenches. Absent on more fashion-forward cuts. The storm shield is functional and visual.

**D-rings.** The metal D-rings on the belt for attaching accessories. Present on most traditional trenches. The D-rings are not decorative. The D-rings are functional. The D-rings age with the trench.

**Gun flap.** The flap across the right shoulder. Originally a place to hold a rifle. Now a decorative detail. Present on most traditional trenches. The gun flap is the detail that makes a trench look like a trench.

**Removable lining.** Almost universal on traditional trenches. The lining extends the season. The lining is also the part that wears out first. The removable lining can be replaced. The replaceable lining extends the trench life.

## How to care for it

A trench is one of the easiest coats to maintain.

**Hang on a wooden hanger.** The shoulders need support. The wooden hanger prevents the shoulders from distorting. The plastic or wire hanger distorts the shoulders over time.

**Brush after wearing.** A soft brush removes surface dirt. The brush also restores the nap of the fabric. The brushing takes thirty seconds. The brushing prevents the need for dry cleaning.

**Dry clean once a year.** Most trenches need dry cleaning once per season at most. More frequent cleaning wears the fabric. Less frequent cleaning lets stains set.

**Re-wax every few years.** Traditional cotton gabardine benefits from re-waxing every three to five years. The re-waxing restores water resistance. The re-waxing also adds character. Burberry offers re-waxing for their trenches. Other brands offer it through cobblers.

**Store unbuttoned.** The buttons should be unbuttoned when the trench is hanging. The unbuttoned trench maintains its shape. The buttoned trench stretches out the buttonholes.

## The honest summary

A trench is the most cost-effective piece in any wardrobe. The math is real. The math is most favorable when the trench is high quality and worn for a decade. The math is least favorable when the trench is cheap and replaced every few years.

The 2026 shortlist has options at every price point. The options at every price point are good. The right answer depends on budget, frequency of wear, and styling preference.

The trench is the kind of piece that earns its place over time. The first season, the trench is just a coat. The fifth season, the trench is part of the wardrobe. The tenth season, the trench is a piece of the wardrobe’s history.

A good trench is not an expense. A good trench is an investment. The investment pays off in cost per wear, in style consistency, and in the simple pleasure of wearing a piece that gets better with age.

## What I learned from owning three

The mistakes and lessons from buying and wearing three trenches over eleven years.

**The first trench was the wrong color.** I bought black for versatility. The black was the wrong answer for my skin tone and my wardrobe. The black made me look washed out. The black also showed every piece of lint and pet hair. The second trench, in honey, was the right color all along. The lesson is to try colors before committing.

**The second trench was the right fit but wrong fabric.** I bought a synthetic blend because it was on sale. The synthetic did not age well. The synthetic pilled. The synthetic lost its shape after one season. The lesson is that the fabric matters as much as the brand.

**The third trench was the right one.** Honey. Cotton gabardine. Burberry Kensington on sale. The trench has been to four countries with me. The trench is the example of what a good trench should be.

The mistake most people make is buying the first trench they see. The mistake costs them years of wearing the wrong coat. The years are what the waiting would have saved. The saved is what the discipline is for.
