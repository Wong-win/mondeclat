I have been buying secondhand for ten years. I have built most of my wardrobe from resale shops, vintage stores, and online platforms. I have learned where to go in every city I visit. I have also learned what to look for at each type of shop.

This is the working map. The map is specific. The map is based on what I have actually bought at each shop. The map is current as of 2026.

## Paris

The most concentrated vintage market in Europe. The selection is the best in the world for designer and high-end vintage. The prices are higher than other European cities. The quality is also higher.

**Kilo Shop, multiple locations.** Sells by weight at €30 to €50 per kilo. The selection is wide. The pieces are mostly fast fashion from previous decades, with some vintage finds. The trick is to go through every rack slowly. The trick is to look at the back of the rack, where the better pieces are hidden. The flagship is at 69 Rue de la Verrerie.

**Free’P’star, multiple locations.** Similar to Kilo Shop. The selection varies by location. The Marais location is the best. The pieces are more curated. The prices are slightly higher than Kilo Shop.

**Thanx God I'm a V.I.P., 18 Rue de Lancry.** The best vintage shop in Paris for designer pieces. The selection is curated. The prices are firm. The authenticity is guaranteed. The shop is small. The shop is worth the visit.

**Le Marais.** The neighborhood has dozens of vintage shops concentrated in a few streets. The walking tour takes an afternoon. The walking tour covers Rue de Sévigné, Rue des Rosiers, Rue Sainte-Croix de la Bretonnerie. The walking tour is the right way to find the smaller shops.

**Puces de Saint-Ouen.** The flea market. The largest vintage market in the world. The market requires a full day. The market is for serious buyers. The market has every era, every style, every price point. The market rewards multiple visits.

## London

The best market for mid-century and British heritage pieces. The selection of Burberry trenches, Aquascutum coats, and Pringle knits is unmatched. The prices for these specific categories are the lowest in Europe.

**Beyond Retro, multiple locations.** The chain has the most consistent quality. The prices are reasonable. The selection rotates quickly. The best location is Cheshire Street in East London. The shop is well-organized by color and size.

**Rellik, 8 Hollywood Road.** The best vintage shop in London for designer pieces. The selection is curated. The owners are knowledgeable. The prices are firm but fair. The shop is small. The shop is worth the visit.

**One of a Kind, 259 Portobello Road.** The best vintage shop on Portobello. The selection is designer and high-end. The prices are the highest on the market. The authenticity is guaranteed. The shop is small but well-curated.

**Portobello Road Market.** The Saturday market is the largest vintage market in the UK. The market is for browsing. The market has every era and every style. The market rewards arriving early (before 9am) and being willing to dig.

**Absolute Vintage, 15 Hanbury Street.** The best for mid-century American vintage. The selection is curated. The prices are reasonable. The shop is small. The shop is worth the visit.

**Kilo Sale, multiple locations.** Sells by weight. The selection varies. The prices are €25 per kilo for clothing. The trick is to go through the entire selection. The flagship is at Van Woustraat.

**Waterlooplein Market.** The daily flea market. The selection is wide. The prices are negotiable. The market is for browsing. The market rewards arriving early.

## What to look for at each type of shop

A few rules I follow.

**Check the seams.** The seams are the best indicator of construction quality. The seams on high-quality pieces are tight, even, and reinforced. The seams on fast fashion pieces are loose and uneven. The seams are also the first thing to fail on a worn piece.

**Check the fabric content.** The fabric content on the label tells you most of what you need to know. Natural fibers (cotton, wool, silk, linen) age well. Synthetic fibers (polyester, acrylic, nylon) age less well. The fiber content also tells you how to care for the piece.

**Check the brand.** The brand tells you the design intent. The brand does not tell you the quality. A high-end brand can produce a low-quality piece. A low-end brand can produce a high-quality piece. The brand is a starting point, not a verdict.

**Check the condition.** The condition tells you the lifespan remaining. The lifespan remaining is the cost per wear. The cost per wear is the metric. The metric is what to optimize for.

**Check the fit.** The fit is the only thing that matters for how the piece will look. The fit can be altered. The fit is also the most expensive thing to get right. The fit is worth the time in the fitting room.

## The honest summary

The secondhand market is the most cost-effective way to build a wardrobe. The secondhand market is also the most fun way to build a wardrobe. The secondhand market rewards knowledge, patience, and willingness to dig.

The map above is the starting point. The map is for five cities I have shopped in extensively. The map is not complete. The map is a working map that I add to with each visit.

The principles are universal. The principles are: go to the neighborhood with the most shops, look at the back of the rack, check the seams, check the fabric content, and try everything on. The principles work in any city.

The savings are real. The savings on a typical secondhand wardrobe versus a new wardrobe are 50 to 80 percent. The quality is often higher. The character is better. The environmental cost is dramatically lower.

The shift from new to secondhand is the single most impactful change I have made to my wardrobe practice. The shift was gradual. The shift is permanent.
