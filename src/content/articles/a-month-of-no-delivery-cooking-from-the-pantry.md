In October 2025, I committed to a full month without food delivery or takeout. The commitment included: no Deliveroo, no Uber Eats, no restaurant delivery, no convenience food from the supermarket. I would cook every meal from what was already in my apartment. I could shop for fresh ingredients once a week at most.

The experiment changed more than my cooking. The experiment changed my relationship with food, my weekly rhythm, and my bank account.

## What I had in my pantry

Before the experiment, I took inventory.

**Pantry:** Rice (jasmine and arborio), dried pasta (three types), canned tomatoes (four cans), canned beans (chickpeas, black beans, cannellini), canned tuna, dried lentils, flour (all-purpose and bread), sugar, salt, oil (olive and neutral), vinegar (white and balsamic), soy sauce, miso, honey, spices (a well-stocked drawer), dried mushrooms, nuts (almonds, walnuts), seeds (sesame, pumpkin), dried fruit (raisins, apricots), chocolate, oats.

**Fridge:** Eggs (about a dozen), butter, parmesan, milk, yogurt, hot sauce, mustard, mayonnaise, a few condiments.

**Freezer:** Frozen peas, frozen spinach, frozen berries, bread (homemade, two loaves), chicken stock (homemade, frozen in containers).

**Fresh produce (at start):** Onions (a bag), garlic, potatoes (a bag), carrots, some soft herbs (parsley, thyme), lemons, a few apples, a butternut squash.

The pantry was well-stocked. The fresh produce would run out by week two. The fridge would last longer. The freezer would last the month.

## What I cooked for 30 days

A rough log of meals.

**Days 1-7:** Pasta with various sauces (tomato, aglio e olio, cacio e pepe), fried rice with frozen vegetables, lentil soup, mushroom risotto, bean stew, vegetable curry, omelets. The cooking was comfortable. The cooking was what I usually make.

**Days 8-14:** The fresh produce ran low. The meals became more rice-and-beans focused. Bean and rice bowls, pasta fagioli, chickpea curry, lentil dal, fried rice variations. The variety came from spices. The spices were the difference.

**Days 15-21:** I bought fresh vegetables once (carrots, cabbage, broccoli, herbs). The cooking shifted toward stir-fries, roasted vegetables, soups. The bread ran out. The bread was replaced by crackers and tortillas I had in the pantry.

**Days 22-30:** The final week was the most creative. The pantry was running low. The meals were simple: bean soups, pasta with whatever vegetables I had, fried rice with eggs, omelets, yogurt with fruit and honey. The simplicity was the point. The simplicity was what I had been avoiding for years.

## What I learned

The lessons that emerged.

**The pantry is more versatile than I thought.** I had everything I needed to cook for 30 days. The everything was not exciting. The everything was sufficient. The sufficiency was the value.

**Spices are the difference.** The same beans can taste like 10 different meals depending on the spice combination. The combinations are: cumin + smoked paprika for Mexican, garam masala + turmeric for Indian, soy + ginger + garlic for Asian, thyme + bay + mustard for French. The combinations are what made the variety possible.

**Rice is the most versatile grain.** I cooked rice at least 20 times in 30 days. The rice was the base for stir-fries, curries, bowls, and sides. The rice is cheap. The rice stores forever. The rice should be a pantry staple for everyone.

**Eggs save you when nothing else works.** The eggs are in every cuisine. The eggs are fast. The eggs are nutritious. The eggs are what saved me on the days I did not feel like cooking.

**Frozen vegetables are underrated.** The frozen peas, spinach, and berries were as nutritious as fresh. The frozen are cheaper. The frozen last longer. The frozen should be in every pantry.

**The shopping list shrinks.** The grocery shopping changed from 3-4 times per week to once per week. The once-per-week shopping is what saved time. The saved time is what the experiment was for.

## What was hard

The parts that were difficult.

**The first three days.** The transition was the hardest part. The transition was the change from takeout convenience to home cooking. The home cooking took more effort. The effort was what I had to overcome.

**Days 12-15.** The middle of the experiment was the hardest. The fresh produce was gone. The pantry was getting low. The variety was reduced. The reduction was what I had to push through.

**Social occasions.** A friend invited me to dinner. I went. I also had a work lunch that week. The work lunch was at a restaurant. The restaurant was not delivery, so it was allowed. The line was what I had to think about.

**The temptation.** The pull of the delivery apps was real. The pull was strongest at the end of a long day. The long day was when cooking felt impossible. The impossible feeling was what I had to override.

## What surprised me

The unexpected findings.

**I lost 2 kilograms.** The weight loss came from eating less and cooking more. The cooking is more nutritious. The nutrition is what made the difference. The difference was not the goal. The goal was the discipline. The discipline produced the side benefit.

**I saved €340.** The savings were substantial. The savings came from no delivery fees, no impulse orders, and less grocery shopping. The savings are what convinced me to continue the practice.

**I cooked more creatively.** The creativity came from having fewer options. The fewer options forced experimentation. The experimentation produced new favorite meals. The favorites are still in my rotation.

**I ate more vegetables.** The vegetable intake went up. The increase came from using frozen vegetables more and planning meals around what I had. The planning is what made the difference.

**My energy stabilized.** The energy on day felt different. The difference came from eating at regular times, eating more protein, and not having the blood sugar spikes from takeout. The stabilization was the most surprising benefit.

**My sleep improved.** The sleep improvement came from lighter dinners and less late-night eating. The improvement was small. The improvement was meaningful.

## What I changed permanently

The practices I kept.

**One delivery per month.** I allow myself one delivery per month now. The once-per-month delivery is what I use for social occasions. The social occasions are what I allow it for. The allowance is what keeps the practice sustainable.

**Bigger pantry.** The pantry is now larger. The larger pantry is the result of intentional shopping. The shopping focuses on shelf-stable items I actually use. The use is what determines the shopping.

**Less fresh shopping.** The fresh grocery shopping is once per week now. The once-per-week shopping is what I do. The doing is what I learned. The learning was the experiment.

**More batch cooking.** The batch cooking is what I do on weekends. The weekend cooking produces lunches for the week. The lunches are what save me from the delivery temptation.

**Fewer restaurant meals.** The restaurant meals are down to 2-3 per month. The reduction was not the goal. The reduction is what happened. The happening is what the experiment produced.

## What I would not give up

A few things that came back after the experiment.

**The variety of fresh produce.** The fresh produce is back to regular shopping. The shopping is what I do once a week. The once-a-week is what produces the variety.

**The occasional takeout.** The takeout is now once per month. The once-a-month is what I allow. The allowance is for social occasions. The occasions are what I use it for.

**The Friday dinner out.** The Friday dinner out is what I do most weeks. The dinner is at a restaurant. The restaurant is for social reasons. The social is what the dinner is for.

## What I recommend

If you want to try a no-delivery month.

**Plan ahead.** The week before, build your pantry. The pantry should have rice, pasta, beans, canned tomatoes, spices, oil, vinegar. The basics are what you need.

**Start small.** The first attempt can be one week, not a month. The one-week is the start. The start is what produces the habit. The habit is what produces the change.

**Track what you spend.** The tracking reveals the savings. The savings are motivating. The motivation is what produces the next month.

**Allow exceptions.** The exceptions are for social occasions. The occasions are what you should not skip. The skipping is what makes the experiment unsustainable.

**Find recipes that excite you.** The exciting recipes are what make the cooking enjoyable. The enjoyment is what makes the practice sustainable. The sustainability is what produces the change.

## The honest summary

The no-delivery month was one of the more impactful lifestyle experiments I have done. The experiment saved money, improved health, and changed how I cook.

The experiment is not for everyone. The experiment works for someone who has the time and interest in cooking. The experiment does not work for someone who is in a period of intense work or family demands.

The shift from regular delivery to mostly cooking is permanent for me. The permanence is what the experiment produced. The produced was what I wanted.

The recommendation is to try it. The try is what produces the data. The data is what tells you if the shift is right for you. The right is what matters.

The cooking is the answer. The cooking is what I do most days. The most days are what the experiment produced. The experiment was the start. The start was worth it.
