I had the same skin for thirty years. Combination, slightly oily in the T-zone, clear except for the occasional breakout around my period, prone to a few clogged pores on my chin. I used whatever cleanser was on sale and a basic moisturizer. I did not think about my skin. My skin did not think about me.

Then I turned 34 and everything changed. The oil disappeared. The breakouts did not. The texture got rougher. The fine lines around my eyes that I had not noticed in my twenties appeared overnight. My skin, which had been doing its job quietly for three decades, started making demands.

This is the story of the year I spent figuring out what was happening, the things I tried that did not work, the things that did, and the routine I have now — which is more involved than anything I would have believed at 25 but which has made my skin better at 36 than it was at 26.

## What changed, biologically

The honest version first. Skin aging is not one thing. It is a constellation of changes that accelerate in different decades.

In your thirties, the most relevant changes are:

**Cell turnover slows.** Your skin replaces itself every 28 days in your twenties, every 35 to 40 days in your thirties, and every 45 to 50 days in your forties. Slower turnover means dead skin cells accumulate on the surface, which makes the texture rougher, the tone duller, and the pores look larger.

**Collagen production drops.** Approximately 1 percent per year after age 25, accelerating after 35. The structural support that kept skin firm starts to thin. The visible effects in the thirties are usually subtle — fine lines around the eyes, slight laxity around the jaw, loss of the cheekbone highlight that was there in your twenties.

**Sebum production changes.** Most people see oiliness decrease. Some people see it increase. Both responses are normal. The mechanism is hormonal — estrogen decline and its interaction with androgen production.

**Hydration decreases.** The skin's natural hyaluronic acid content drops, the lipid barrier weakens, and the skin becomes less efficient at retaining water. The visible result is dryness, flakiness, and a crepe-like texture in areas that were never dry before.

None of this is fixable by a single product. The reason the thirties require a different routine is that the skin is doing different things, and the routine has to address all of them.

## What I tried that did not work

I went through a phase in early 2024 where I tried almost everything. Some of it was useful. A lot of it was not.

**The 12-step routine.** I tried it. It did not make my skin better. It made my skin more sensitive, more reactive, and harder to read. When everything is on the skin, you cannot tell which product is helping and which is hurting. This is the fundamental problem with elaborate routines: they are designed to be optimized, but the optimization requires more variables than you can track.

**The trending actives.** I tried The Ordinary's full lineup at various points. The glycolic toner made my skin sting. The niacinamide did nothing. The lactic acid was fine. The aha/bha peel made my face peel for a week. The conclusion I came to was that high-street actives are useful but require careful introduction, and the "everything at once" approach the internet pushes is not the right way to use them.

**The expensive moisturizer.** I tried La Mer. It is a €190 moisturizer. It is a very good moisturizer. It is not €190 better than the €30 CeraVe I now use.

**The "miracle" serum.** I tried Vitamin C, hyaluronic acid, peptides, growth factors, snail mucin, propolis, bakuchiol, and various other ingredients the internet told me were essential. Some helped. Most did not. The problem is not that any of these are bad ingredients. The problem is that I did not have a framework for figuring out which ones my skin needed.

## The framework I built

After about a year of trying things, I developed a framework that I wish I had started with. It is five questions, in order:

**What is my skin actually doing right now?** Not what it was doing last year, not what it will be doing next year. What is happening today. Is it dry? Oily? Breaking out? Sensitive? Reactive? Pigmented? Texture off? The answer changes weekly, sometimes daily.

**What is the simplest intervention that addresses it?** The simpler the better. Dry skin does not need a serum. It needs a better moisturizer. Breakouts do not need an acid. They need a non-comedogenic routine. The simplest intervention is the one with the fewest side effects.

**Can I tell whether it is working?** If you cannot measure the outcome, you cannot tell whether the product is helping. The measurable outcomes are: oiliness (visual), breakouts (count), texture (visual and tactile), hydration (visual and tactile), fine lines (visible under consistent lighting). Vague outcomes like "glow" are not measurable.

**Is the cost justified by the outcome?** This is the question the beauty industry does not want you to ask. A €30 product that works is a better product than a €200 product that works equally well. A €200 product that works better than a €30 product is a €200 product worth buying only if the difference matters to you.

**Am I using it consistently?** A product you use every day for three years is a better product than one you use once and forget. Consistency is the variable that matters most.

The framework forces clarity. It is not a prescription. It is a way of thinking about what your skin needs and whether what you are doing is addressing it.

## The routine I have now

Twelve months into the framework, my routine is this. It is not aspirational. It is what works.

### Morning

1. Rinse with water (no cleanser, since my skin is not producing overnight oil)
2. The Ordinary Hyaluronic Acid 2% + B5 (€8, on damp skin)
3. La Roche-Posay Anthelios UVMune 400 SPF 50+ (€16, the most important product in the routine)
4. CeraVe Moisturizing Cream (€15, used as a light layer if the day is dry)

Total time: 90 seconds. Total cost per day: roughly €1.

### Evening

1. CeraVe Hydrating Cleanser (€12, gentle enough not to strip)
2. The Ordinary Glycolic Acid 7% Toner, three nights a week (€8)
3. La Roche-Posay Retinol B3 Serum, three other nights (€35, alternate nights with the acid)
4. CeraVe Moisturizing Cream (€15)

Total time: three minutes. Total cost per day: roughly €1.50.

### Weekly

Sheet mask if my skin feels dehydrated. The Dr. Jart+ Ceramidin one (€6 per use). Not essential, but useful.

This is the routine. It contains one expensive product (the retinol, at €35) and the rest is the budget tier. Total annual cost is roughly €300, including replacing products. The most expensive thing I do not use is also the most important: I am consistent.

## What this routine actually does

Each product has a specific purpose. None of them is decorative.

**Hyaluronic acid.** Holds water in the skin. The single most effective humectant available. The Ordinary version is €8 and works as well as versions costing five times as much.

**Sunscreen.** The most important anti-aging product that exists. UV exposure is responsible for 80 to 90 percent of visible skin aging. Daily SPF 30 or higher prevents most of it. La Roche-Posay's UVMune 400 is the best broad-spectrum sunscreen I have used because it does not pill under makeup and does not have the white cast of mineral sunscreens.

**CeraVe cleanser and cream.** Contain ceramides, which are the lipids that make up the skin barrier. Both are formulated at a pH that does not disrupt the skin's acid mantle. Both are fragrance-free, which matters more than I appreciated when I was younger.

**Glycolic acid.** Exfoliates dead skin cells, accelerates cell turnover, improves texture and tone. Three nights a week is enough. More than that causes irritation in most skin types.

**Retinol.** Increases cell turnover, stimulates collagen production, addresses fine lines and pigmentation. The active most consistently supported by research. The reason I alternate with the acid is that using both every night causes irritation in most skin types.

## What I learned that I wish I knew at 25

A few things that took me ten years and a lot of money to internalize.

**Sunscreen is everything.** The single most effective anti-aging intervention is daily sunscreen. Everything else is incremental by comparison. If you only adopt one product, adopt this one.

**Consistency beats intensity.** A cheap routine done every day for ten years will outperform an expensive routine done inconsistently. The compounding effect of consistency is the most under-priced variable in skincare.

**More products do not mean better skin.** Most skincare problems are solvable with three to five well-chosen products. The twelve-step routines are marketing. They sell products. They do not produce better skin.

**Fragrance is not your friend.** Fragrance in skincare is the most common cause of irritation, especially in cleansers and moisturizers. Fragrance-free is almost always the better choice.

**"Sensitive skin" is usually "irritated skin."** If your skin reacts to products, it is usually because the products are too aggressive, not because your skin is inherently sensitive. Reduce the active ingredients. Use a barrier-supporting moisturizer. The sensitivity usually resolves.

**The industry is not on your side.** Most skincare marketing is designed to make you buy more products, not to make your skin better. The dermatologist's office is the only place where the advice is consistently aligned with your interests. Get a dermatologist if you can.

## The honest summary

I do not have great skin. I have decent skin for my age, which is a more useful goal. The routine I have now is simple, affordable, and consistent. It is not the routine I would have designed at 25, but at 25 I would not have been disciplined enough to do it.

The skin you have at 40 is mostly the skin you took care of at 30. The skin you have at 50 is mostly the skin you took care of at 40. The compounding effect of consistent care is the only skincare intervention that has been proven over decades. Everything else is incremental.

If you are in your late twenties or early thirties and your skin is starting to change, the time to start is now. The routine I described above will cost you €300 a year and an hour a week. The return is the difference between skin that has been cared for and skin that has not. The compounding effect is permanent.
